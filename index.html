Movement II

Practice

The second movement develops the beat and frequently highlights the drumline. Visually it involves intricate drill or body movements, keeping the energy high but shifting the musical style away from the intro.

Before sunrise, in the autumn fog, a marching band is already on the field practicing. The metronome clicks at 140 BPM. Long before the first bell, you show up ready, because the future performance demands it.

That same discipline and dedication, line by line - point by point, is how I have approached engineering from day one.

The Mobile Switching Office I built started from a gravel lot. Trunk lines ordered, foundation poured, a secure room built within a building to house the racks, power, and redundancy that a live network requires. Switches, Voice Mail Systems, Base Station Controllers, HVAC… every component installed, integrated, and rigorously tested before a single call could be made.

Like a band that earns its halftime show’s impeccability through thousands of repetitions, that foundation earned me Nextel’s Circle of Excellence award and a promotion to a National Technical Support Engineer.

From there, the stakes only grew. A telecom outage costs millions per hour and disrupts services that people’s lives depend on. On September 11th, 2001, Nextel’s dispatch network was one of the few communication channels still working for first responders. We provided real-time status updates to police, FBI, NSA, and DHS.

I supported that national network 24/7, managing outages from triage to root cause, coordinating vendors and cross-functional teams, and building internal tooling that improved how the entire team operated.

In marching band, the seniors make it look effortless. What you don’t see are the years of 6am practices that got them there, and the skillset they have honed.

Someone who stays composed when the systems go down and every second counts. Someone who can diagnose a problem they’ve never seen before, with incomplete information, while stakeholders are waiting on the line. Someone who can lead a bridge call with vendors, engineers, and federal agencies without losing the thread. And someone who never stops learning, because technology never stops changing.

Like marching band, through practice, I’ve learned to perform when it matters most.

On Monday, November 9th, 2026, Applications AE Brian Roche sent a Slack message at 4:17pm: “Hey man, have an emergency, can you join a Toyota outage call?” Toyota had been down since 8am, and on the outage bridge since 10am.

Toyota dealers rely on Tableau dashboards embedded in Salesforce Experience Cloud to run their business — accessed thousands of times per hour across their entire dealer network. Six hours in, the team still had no resolution.

By 5:17pm, I had identified and resolved the Tableau Server misconfiguration that had brought it all down. Dealer operations restored.

Sr. Manager of Technical Support Emily Cowan reached out afterward: “Thank you for jumping on today, really really appreciate it.”
